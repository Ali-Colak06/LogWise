HOW TO USE IT

Volume is the amount of work you repeat and recover from.

Training volume is a useful signal when you compare the same exercise over time. A bigger number is not automatically better, but it helps explain why a week felt easy, productive, or too hard to recover from.

  1. Use working sets. Warm-ups can stay out unless you intentionally want them in your total.
  2. Keep units consistent. Compare kg weeks with kg weeks, or lb weeks with lb weeks.
  3. Review by exercise. Total session volume is helpful, but the per-exercise summary shows where the work actually went.

Volume formula

Per row: volume = sets x reps x weight

Total: add every row's volume together.

Example: 3 sets x 8 reps x 80 kg = 1,920 kg of bench press volume.

FAQ

Workout volume questions

How do you calculate workout volume?

Multiply sets by reps by weight for each exercise row, then add the rows together. For example, 3 x 8 x 80 kg is 1,920 kg of volume.

Should I include warm-up sets?

Usually, no. Track working-set volume first because it is easier to compare. Include warm-ups only if you have a specific reason.

Is more volume always better?

No. More volume can help when you can recover from it and performance is improving. If reps, load, or energy drop, the volume may be too high.
