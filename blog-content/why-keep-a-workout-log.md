Most people who train consistently fall into the same trap: they show up every week, but they do not remember **exactly what they lifted last week**. If the answer to "was my bench 60 kg or 65 kg?" is a guess, progress becomes a guess too.

## Memory is a poor training partner

Your brain is not built to store every set, rep, and load detail. It also tends to remember past performance a little more generously than reality. That means if you do not keep a log, you may think you are progressing faster than you actually are.

A workout log removes the fog. The number is there. It either moved up, stayed flat, or moved down.

## Progressive overload needs a record

The basic rule behind strength and muscle gain is simple: ask your body to do **a little more than last time**. That is progressive overload.

But "more than last time" only works if you know what last time was:

- Last week: Squat 80 kg x 8, 8, 7
- This week: aim for 80 kg x 8, 8, **8** — or move to 82.5 kg if the work felt strong

Without a log, the comparison disappears. With a log, every workout starts with a clear target.

## You spot plateaus earlier

Progress is never a straight line. Still, there is a big difference between seeing that a lift has been flat for four weeks and vaguely feeling that training has slowed down.

When you have data, your response can be specific: add volume, take a deload, change a variation, or improve recovery. Without data, you are guessing.

## How to start in three steps

1. **Log every working set** — exercise, weight, and reps. Add RIR if you want more context.
2. **Check your last entry before the lift** — today's target is one small step above it.
3. **Review once per week** — is the trend moving up, flat, or down?

You do not need a perfect system to begin. A notebook works. A spreadsheet works. But your phone is already with you, and [LogWise](https://logwise.fit/app) is built for this exact job: fast set logging, automatic charts, and PR tracking without setup.

> A workout log is not for remembering. It is for **comparing**.

Log your first set today. Three months from now, you will have evidence instead of a feeling.
