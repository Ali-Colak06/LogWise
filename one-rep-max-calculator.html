HOW TO USE IT

Turn one hard set into a useful training number.

Use a recent set where your form stayed consistent. Enter the weight and reps, then use the estimate to plan working sets, compare progress, or track PRs over time.

  1. Pick a clean set. Sets of 2 to 10 reps usually give the most useful estimate.
  2. Choose the formula. LogWise uses Epley in the app; Brzycki is included here for comparison.
  3. Track the trend. A single estimate is less important than whether your estimated 1RM rises over weeks.

1RM formula

Epley: 1RM = weight × (1 + reps / 30)

Brzycki: 1RM = weight × 36 / (37 - reps)

All one rep max formulas are estimates. Use them to guide training, not as a guarantee of what you can safely lift today.

FAQ

Common 1RM questions

How accurate is a one rep max calculator?

It is an estimate. Accuracy depends on rep range, technique, fatigue, and how close the set was to failure. It is most useful as a trend over time.

What rep range should I use?

Use a challenging set of 2 to 10 clean reps when possible. Very high-rep sets can make estimates less reliable.

Should I test a true max?

You do not need to test a true max often to train well. Estimated 1RM is usually enough for planning, especially for general strength and hypertrophy training.
